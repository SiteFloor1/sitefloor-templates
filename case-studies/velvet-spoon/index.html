Case study · Chocolatier and gelateria

Lift the lid. Every layer is a plan.

Velvet Spoon is a fictional chocolatier on London Road, Leicester, made to show Sitefloor's dessert kit. The site is set out like the shop's own box: each piece is something it does for the shop, and each layer goes one plan deeper.

      On a phone

      It opens on the pour, then the counter.

      The first thing a customer sees is the shop itself: its film, then today's tubs with their prices. When the raspberry sorbet runs out, the tub stays on the wall and says "Back tomorrow". The shop changes one cell in a Google Sheet on its phone; the page does the rest.

        By post

        It knows when the post goes.

        Customers ask "will it be there for Friday?". The page answers from the shop's own posting days and noon cut-off, and it skips Sundays, bank holidays, Christmas and any day the shop is closed. It always says "usually", because no courier promises the next day.

          The box

          Fill it, tie it, write the card.

          The shop's own chocolates, from its own photographs, placed one by one. A piece that sold out today can't go in. When the box is full the ribbon ties with the shop's seal, and the card is written on the lid. The order arrives on WhatsApp with every piece, the price and the allergens.

          The gift link

          For someone else, it arrives as a link.

          Tick "It's a gift" and the customer gets a message to forward. It opens a page with their box, the card, who it's from and when to collect it. Nothing is stored on a server: the box travels inside the link itself, and a link the shop wouldn't sell simply doesn't open.

          Maya, there's a box waiting for you at Velvet Spoon, today, 8pm. From Sam.velvetspoon.co.uk/gift#…

          Your seal · try it

          Their initials, on the shop's own seal.

          For wedding favours and company gifts, the couple or the company types two letters and a line, and the seal on the lid is redrawn as they type. It goes with the request, so the shop knows exactly what to print.

          &

          The advent box · try it

          Twenty-four mornings.

          The windows sit in shuffled places, like a printed calendar, with one of the shop's chocolates behind each. Before December every window opens, so customers can see inside before they order. From the 1st, one more opens each morning, by the shop's date in the UK, wherever the visitor is.

          Open any window.

          The tasting flight

          Five pieces, in the order to taste them.

          The customer chooses; the board sorts them lightest to darkest, with the shop's own line on each. A card to keep at the counter, and an order for collection.
