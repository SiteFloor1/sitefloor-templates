    L01Hall · Home page · Signature

    One keypad, three evenings.

    The first thing anyone sees is the house itself, at dusk, edge to edge. Beside it sit three engraved keys: Evening, Cinema and Away. Each one swaps the photograph for the same house in that scene, with one line saying what the scene does.

    It sells what an electrician actually leaves behind. Nobody buys cable; they buy a house that looks like this at eight o'clock.

    L03Hall · Home page · Signature

    Light the house, room by room.

    Further down the home page, a whole house in 3D waits in the dark, its garden and pool lit. One tap and it comes on: the landing first, then the living room, the kitchen, the glass garden room, the floor above, the cinema below. Lights warm as they dim, the way good fittings do.

    Every light drawn is one Corbel lists, at Corbel's own prices. Tap a room to light it and the camera goes there. The total of everything lit sits in the corner, ready to send on WhatsApp. Nothing heavy loads until the tap.

    L02Hall · Home page · Plus

    Every light has a reason.

    A finished room, with each light numbered on the photograph. Tap a number and the fitting, why it's there, its scene, its colour and how it's controlled appear beside it, in Corbel's words.

    It's the conversation a lighting designer has on a first visit, already had. People arrive knowing why a wall glows instead of a ceiling glaring.

    Below it, the rooms at night (a kitchen, a living room, a cinema, a bathroom), each with its scene and what was done. (Every light's reason on Plus; the lit rooms on Starter, as L04.)

    L06Hall · Home page · Starter

    Something's wrong? Tell us this.

    The guide never says how to fix anything. Pick what happened and it lists what Corbel needs to hear when you call, with one safety line, and the message is ready for WhatsApp. The emergency number sits above every page.

    The site won't take a repair instruction: wording that tells someone to reset, swap or switch something back on is refused before it can be published.

    What to tell us when you call

      L10L11Garage · Car chargers

      Will a charger go on my drive?

      Four answers give a straight verdict: a standard install at Corbel's price, a survey first and why, or not as a home install. A street space is never a yes. The grant link goes to gov.uk and never names an amount. (Plus.)

      On Signature the drive comes in 3D: slide the charger along the wall and the cable from the fuse board, the lead to the car's port and the price all follow, from Corbel's own figures.

      L12 · L13L14Study · Your project

      The whole house, roughly.

      Before anyone visits, a range from Corbel's own figures. The kind of home, the bedrooms and the work give a low and a high, rounded outwards, never a single price. (Signature.)

      Beside it, how a job runs from first visit to certificates, and "Before you call": four answers and a photo of the fuse board in one tidy WhatsApp message, so the first visit can bring a quote. (Plus.)

      A room, designed on the phone.

      Choose a room, its size and the layers of light, and the fittings and the price come from Corbel's own list, with a keypad added once there's more than one layer. Under it, a fader moves between the client's own photographs of one room, low to full.

      The projects page files every finished job by kind of work, each with where it is and what was done, so a period house finds the period houses.

      L05 · L17L16Board, porch

      The paperwork, and the way in.

      The services read as the chart inside a fuse board's door, each circuit opening to what's included, with the registers beside them, each linked to the scheme's own public register (Starter). A homeowner's certificates come up by the code Corbel sent, with the next inspection and a reminder for the calendar (Signature). Plan your visit reads like a schedule, the week drawn as a time switch (Starter).
