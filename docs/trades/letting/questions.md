# Independent letting agents: customer questions

What tenants and landlords ask an independent letting agent in England before they get in touch. Drawn from the
public FAQ, "guide to renting", landlord-fees and "how to let" pages of UK national and independent agents, the
booking and enquiry forms on their sites, and the themes that recur in their Google and Trustpilot reviews (slow
repairs, deposit returns, being kept informed, hidden costs). Rules are from GOV.UK, legislation.gov.uk and NTSELAT
(National Trading Standards Estate and Letting Agency Team), checked 15 Nov 2026. England only: Wales and Scotland
have their own rules and the kit does not cover them.

## Tenants

| # | Question | What the agent's site must answer |
|---|---|---|
| T1 | What do you have to rent right now, and is it still available? | Homes from the agent's sheet with status (available, let agreed) and the date the sheet was updated; nothing shown as available once the sheet is stale |
| T2 | How much is it, all in? What's the council tax band, the deposit, the EPC? | Rent per month, holding deposit, tenancy deposit, council tax band and EPC rating on every home (NTSELAT material information, Part A) |
| T3 | What else should I know: bills, broadband, mobile signal, parking, which floor, lift, garden? | The rest of the material information no more than one click from the listing (NTSELAT Parts B and C) |
| T4 | Can I see it? When are viewings, can I come at the weekend, can someone show me round on a video call? | Viewing request or the agent's open viewing slots; video viewings if the agent offers them |
| T5 | What will I pay before I move in? Are there any fees? | Only the permitted payments: holding deposit (max 1 week's rent), tenancy deposit (max 5 weeks' rent under £50,000 a year, 6 weeks' from £50,000 to £100,000), first month's rent after signing (max 1 month). No admin, referencing or check-in fees |
| T6 | How does the holding deposit work, and when would I lose it? | Max 1 week's rent per tenancy, one at a time, 15 calendar days to agree unless agreed otherwise in writing; when it can be kept (e.g. false information, failing a Right to Rent check, pulling out) |
| T7 | Where is my deposit kept, and how do I get it back? | The government-approved deposit protection scheme the agent or landlord uses; how check-out and deductions work (fair wear and tear) |
| T8 | What referencing do you do? What income do I need? Can I use a guarantor? | The agent's own affordability rule and referencing steps; guarantors and guarantor services allowed |
| T9 | Can I have a pet? Do you accept tenants with children or on benefits? | Pet requests considered (refused only for a valid reason); no refusal because of children or benefits (Renters' Rights Act 2025, from 1 May 2026) |
| T10 | How long is the tenancy? Can I leave early? Will the rent go up? | New tenancies are assured periodic (rolling) with no end date; rent can rise at most once a year with 2 months' notice (Form 4A, section 13) |
| T11 | Will you take offers above the asking rent? | No: the asking rent must be published and offers above it can't be encouraged or accepted (from 1 May 2026) |
| T12 | Who do I call when something breaks? What counts as an emergency? Out of hours? | Repair reporting (what to tell us, photos), the emergency line, what the agent does vs the landlord, for managed homes |
| T13 | Is the agent properly protected? Who do I complain to? | Redress scheme and client money protection scheme named on the fees page; the agent's complaints procedure |

## Landlords

| # | Question | What the agent's site must answer |
|---|---|---|
| L1 | What do you charge, including VAT? | Each service's fee (let only, rent collection, fully managed) and every other charge, with VAT, in one list (Consumer Rights Act 2015 s83) |
| L2 | What will I actually receive each month? | The agent's own fee rates applied to a rent the landlord types: an illustration, not advice, and never a promise of rent |
| L3 | What's the difference between your services, and what's included? | What each service does, side by side (marketing, referencing, rent collection, inspections, repairs, compliance, deposit) |
| L4 | What rent could my home get? Will you value it? | A rental valuation request (by the agent, in person or by video); no automated estimate |
| L5 | How fast will you find a tenant? How do you reference them? | The agent's process, step by step, without invented figures (days-to-let or occupancy only if the agent supplies them) |
| L6 | Is my money safe with you? | Client money protection scheme named (certificate linked); redress scheme named; professional body if any |
| L7 | What do I legally need to let (gas safety, EICR, EPC, smoke and CO alarms, deposit protection, Right to Rent, licensing)? | A plain checklist linked to GOV.UK pages, dated, with which items the agent arranges under each service |
| L8 | What has changed with the Renters' Rights Act? | A short, dated summary from GOV.UK (periodic tenancies, section 21 abolished, rent increases once a year, asking rent, pets) linking to the source |
| L9 | Which areas do you cover, and do you know my street? | Areas covered, with the agent's own notes; postcode checker |
| L10 | Who will look after my property, and how will I hear from you? | Named contact, inspections, statements, how repairs are approved |
| L11 | Can you take over a tenancy that's already running? | Switching agent: the steps and what the agent needs |
| L12 | What do other landlords say? | Real reviews only (copied from Google with name and date) |

## The rules the kit follows (England)

- **Tenant Fees Act 2019, as amended by the Renters' Rights Act 2025 (from 1 May 2026).** A landlord or agent may only
  require: rent (once the tenancy agreement is signed; at most one month in advance, taken after signing and before the
  start date); a refundable tenancy deposit (up to 5 weeks' rent if the annual rent is under £50,000, up to 6 weeks' from
  £50,000 to £100,000); a refundable holding deposit (up to 1 week's rent per tenancy, one at a time, 15 calendar days by
  default); up to £50 for a tenant-requested change (more only if justified); a payment for ending early or with too little
  notice (no more than the rent lost); utilities, communication services, TV licence and council tax; a late-rent fee only
  once rent is 2 weeks overdue, at no more than 3 percentage points above the Bank of England base rate on the overdue
  amount; a lost key or fob at its reasonable, evidenced cost; damages for actual, evidenced loss. Nothing else.
  Source: GOV.UK, "Fees you can charge as part of a tenancy" (updated 12 Nov 2026).
- **Showing fees.** Agents must publish their fees on their website, and adverts on third-party sites (Rightmove, Zoopla,
  Facebook) must show them or link to that list (same GOV.UK page). Consumer Rights Act 2015 s83: the list of relevant
  fees (with VAT) goes with a statement naming the redress scheme and, where the agent must belong to one, the client
  money protection scheme (legislation.gov.uk, s83, current to Feb 2026).
- **Redress and client money protection.** Letting and property management agents must belong to a government-approved
  redress scheme (penalty up to £5,000) and, if they hold client money, a client money protection scheme (penalty up to
  £30,000); they must tell clients in writing within 14 days if CMP membership is revoked (council Trading Standards
  guidance summarising the 2014 Order and 2019 Regulations; RBKC page checked 15 Nov 2026).
- **Material information (NTSELAT).** Part A on the listing itself: rent, deposit and holding deposit, council tax band.
  Parts B and C (property type and construction, rooms, utilities, broadband, mobile signal, parking, floor; then
  building safety, restrictions, flood risk, accessibility and the like where they apply) no more than one click away.
  The EPC is a separate legal duty and shows on every listing. Guidance: NTSELAT "Material Information in Property
  Listings (Lettings)", v1.0, Nov 2023. One library lists that version as withdrawn: recheck NTSELAT's current version
  before the build.
- **Renters' Rights Act 2025, phase 1 (in force 1 May 2026).** Assured periodic tenancies with no end date; section 21
  abolished; rent rises once a year with Form 4A and 2 months' notice; an asking rent must be published and bidding above
  it is banned; no discrimination against tenants with children or on benefits; tenants may ask to keep a pet, refused
  only for a valid reason. Source: GOV.UK, "Renters' Rights Act: an overview for landlords". Later phases (landlord
  database, ombudsman) have no fixed start date the kit relies on: the kit says nothing about them until GOV.UK gives one.
