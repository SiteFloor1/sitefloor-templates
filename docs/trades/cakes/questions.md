# Celebration cake designers: customer questions

What this trade's customers ask before they get in touch. How to read it and the questions every trade gets: [`docs/CUSTOMER-QUESTIONS.md`](../../CUSTOMER-QUESTIONS.md).

Commission studios (wedding and celebration cakes made to order), not bakeries selling daily stock (that is the `bakery` kit).
Gathered 5 Nov 2026 from: a Yorkshire couture studio's FAQ and ordering page, a home studio's FAQ, a Peckham studio's wedding
page, a Malvern studio's FAQ, a Cornwall studio's terms and advice page, an anonymous UK studio's general information page,
a Bexley studio's wedding page, a London cake shop's wedding range, and two wedding-directory listings with reviews (a
Belgravia studio, a Hull studio). Figures below are what those sources state; a client's site always uses the client's own.

### Allergens: what the law asks (FSA guidance, checked 5 Nov 2026)
The FSA's business guidance now sits on gov.uk (the food.gov.uk addresses redirect there). The allergens page states
only this, and the client confirms its own kitchen answers:
- The 14 allergens: celery, cereals containing gluten, crustaceans, eggs, fish, lupin, milk, molluscs, mustard,
  peanuts, sesame, soya, sulphites (above 10 ppm), tree nuts.
  ([allergen guidance for food businesses](https://www.gov.uk/government/publications/allergen-guidance-for-food-businesses/allergen-guidance-for-food-businesses))
- Ordered remotely (website, WhatsApp, phone): allergen information must be available before the order is placed and
  again when the cake is delivered (same page, distance selling).
- A cake made to the customer's order is not "prepacked for direct sale", so Natasha's Law labelling doesn't apply;
  the non-prepacked and distance-selling rules do
  ([PPDS guidance for event caterers](https://www.gov.uk/government/publications/prepacked-for-direct-sale-ppds-allergen-labelling-changes-for-event-caterers/prepacked-for-direct-sale-ppds-allergen-labelling-changes-for-event-caterers)).
- "May contain" only after a risk assessment, naming the allergen, never next to a free-from claim for the same allergen
  ([precautionary allergen labelling](https://www.gov.uk/government/publications/precautionary-allergen-labelling/precautionary-allergen-labelling)).
- Baking from home counts: register with the local authority at least 28 days before trading; it's free
  ([starting a food business](https://www.gov.uk/guidance/starting-a-food-business)).
The site never says a cake is "allergen-free" or "safe for" an allergy unless the client gives that wording.
